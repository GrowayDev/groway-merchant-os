     7→## 1. Purpose, scope and status
     8→
     9→**Purpose.** Define a buildable architecture, DevOps model and phased build plan for a credit decisioning, ledger and repayment platform owned by Groway, starting with Groway's own merchant, payment and connectivity ecosystem and extending to third-party partners.
    10→
    11→**In scope.** South Africa first, Malawi second, with jurisdiction as configuration. Credit products, decisioning, ledger, repayment, collections, compliance services, lender and capital management, the SmartPole edge plane, operations tooling and the platform stack.
    12→
    13→**Out of scope for build waves.** Quantum inference, QKD, CBDC settlement and causal graph inference are tracked on a research horizon with explicit triggers (section 14). Groway balance-sheet lending is a decision to be taken in Wave 0, not assumed.
    14→
    15→**Status.** Architecture-level specification. Section 20 aligns it to the Groway source code and overrides sections 8, 9 and 15 where they conflict. Items marked *VERIFY* need confirmation by counsel, the regulator or an audit of existing Groway services before commitment.

    30→## 3. Design principles
    31→
    32→1. **First-party data before partner data.** Prove underwriting on data Groway already owns.
    33→2. **The ledger is the truth.** No partner balance or mutable column is authoritative.
    34→3. **Consent is a runtime control**, enforced at ingestion and at inference, not a checkbox.
    35→4. **Event-sourced core, lean infrastructure.** Design for events; deploy the simplest stack that meets the SLO.
    36→5. **Every decision is explainable**, and every adverse decision has a reason and a human review path.
    37→6. **Offline is first-class at the edge**, within conservative limits.
    38→7. **Jurisdiction is configuration**: policy, limits, reporting and data residency are pluggable per country.
    39→8. **Reuse before build.** Audit existing Groway services before writing new ones (section 15).
    40→9. **Compliance as code**, enforced in CI/CD and at runtime.
    41→10. **Scale triggers, not dates.** Heavier components are adopted when a measured threshold is crossed.

    68→## 6. Reference architecture
    69→

    71→
    72→Requests flow down through the gateway into the event-driven core, every service writes to the data layer, and edge nodes sync signed envelopes down and events up.

    74→### 6.1 Channels and gateway
    75→
    76→| Component | Specification |
    77→| --- | --- |
    78→| API gateway | Kong or APISIX; OpenAPI 3.x; OAuth 2.0 / JWT; schema validation; per-tenant rate limits |
    79→| Identity | Keycloak (OIDC); customer, merchant, agent, partner and staff realms; MFA for staff and agents |
    80→| Policy | OPA at the gateway and in the decision orchestrator; ABAC for tenant, role and purpose |
    81→| Channels | Existing Merchant OS client, and the GoPay client once it is live; WhatsApp commerce layer; USSD and voice fallback; agent app with offline mode |
    82→| Tenancy | Namespace-per-partner on Kubernetes; Postgres row-level security; lender-scoped views |
    83→
    84→### 6.2 Consent and privacy service
    85→
    86→- Append-only consent event store: grant, revoke, purpose, scope, expiry, jurisdiction.
    87→- Purpose tokens checked by OPA at ingestion and at feature retrieval; revocation propagates to the feature store within one sync cycle (target under 5 minutes online, next sync at the edge).
    88→- A signal collected for one purpose (for example WiFi billing) cannot feed underwriting without a new consent event.
    89→- Data subject access, correction and deletion workflows are built here, not retrofitted. *VERIFY against POPIA and Malawi requirements.*
    90→
    91→### 6.3 Signal ingestion
    92→
    93→| Source | Mode | Wave |
    94→| --- | --- | --- |
    95→| Merchant OS sales and inventory | Internal events | 1 |
    96→| GoPay transactions and settlements | Internal events (Stitch and Adumo rail data) | After GoPay go-live |
    97→| Grant+ payment history | Internal events | 1 |
    98→| SmartPole WiFi purchases and session data | Internal events from hub systems | 2 |
    99→| Bureau data | API pull with consent | 1 |
   100→| Partner MNO, ISP, utility | Webhook, polling or file-drop adapters, one container per protocol | 3 |
   101→
   102→All sources pass through a normalisation step that validates against a Protobuf schema, tokenises PII, tags jurisdiction and purpose, and emits a unified temporal event.
   103→
   104→### 6.4 Feature store
   105→
   106→| Tier | Technology | Latency target | Contents |
   107→| --- | --- | --- | --- |
   108→| Hot | Redis | under 10 ms p99 | Latest behavioural features, current limits, session context |
   109→| Warm | PostgreSQL (TimescaleDB if needed) | under 50 ms p99 | 7, 30 and 90 day aggregates, cohort deviations |
   110→| Cold | Object storage with Iceberg tables, DuckDB or Trino | seconds to minutes | Training data, audit reconstruction |
   111→
   112→Rule: every feature needed at decision time is precomputed. No synchronous external calls inside the decision path except bureau where contractually fast and cached.
   113→
   114→### 6.5 Decisioning
   115→
   116→| Component | Specification |
   117→| --- | --- |
   118→| Decision orchestrator | Event-driven service; consumes decision requests, fetches features, runs policy and models, emits decision and full trace; thin synchronous wrapper at the gateway using correlation IDs |
   119→| Credit policy engine | Rules (GoRules or Drools) holding eligibility, hard stops, limits and pricing bands per product and jurisdiction; versioned and approved through change control |
   120→| Affordability | Explicit affordability assessment step per product, with stored inputs and result; mandatory before any credit offer in regulated products *VERIFY NCA requirements* |
   121→| Models | Start with scorecards and gradient-boosted models served via BentoML or KServe; champion/challenger with a 10% canary and a minimum observation window per product |
   122→| Explainability | Reason codes from policy and model attributions; counterfactual guidance (what would change the outcome) delivered in plain language by every channel |
   123→| Human review | Queue for declines above a value threshold, disputed outcomes and policy exceptions; reviewer decision is a ledgered event |
   124→| Automated-decision rules | Where law restricts solely automated decisions, the review path and customer notice are enforced by policy *VERIFY POPIA s71* |

   126→### 6.5.1 Risk and compliance services
   127→
   128→| Service | Specification |
   129→| --- | --- |
   130→| KYC and AML | ID verification vendor integration, FICA-aligned risk rating, sanctions and PEP screening, ongoing monitoring, case management; goAML reporting hooks |
   131→| Bureau | Integration with at least two bureaus; consented pulls, caching, and result storage; contributes repayment data back where permitted |
   132→| Fraud | Device and behaviour signals, velocity rules, merchant collusion checks, synthetic identity checks; fraud decisions feed policy |
   133→| Model registry | MLflow: model, dataset hash, feature set, validation report, approver, deployment record |

   135→### 6.6 Credit lifecycle: ledger, repayment, collections, disputes
   136→
   137→**Ledger.** Double-entry, append-only PostgreSQL with materialised balance views and an outbox table for event publication. Accounts per customer, product, lender, partner, fee and suspense. No mutable balance column is authoritative. Daily automated reconciliation against payment rails and lender statements, with exceptions routed to finance operations.
   138→
   139→**Repayment engine.** Waterfall rules per product and jurisdiction, executed at the settlement boundary (for example a GoPay merchant settlement split) or at the instruction boundary (DebiCheck, PayShap). Supports partial payments, early settlement, reversal handling and compensating entries.
   140→
   141→**Collections.** Arrears bucket workflow, outbound messaging via WhatsApp and SMS, promise-to-pay tracking, restructuring, hardship flags, and hand-over rules. Contact frequency and hours follow jurisdiction policy. Grant-linked products carry an affordability floor: repayment cannot reduce a recipient below a configured minimum retained amount.
   142→
   143→**Disputes and complaints.** Case management with service-level clocks, ledger adjustments by approved reversal only, and complaint reporting to regulators as required.
   144→
   145→### 6.7 Lender and capital management
   146→
   147→| Capability | Specification |
   148→| --- | --- |
   149→| Lender onboarding | Lender profile, product allocation, risk appetite, limits and concentration caps |
   150→| Capital allocation | Real-time exposure per lender, product and tenant; allocation rules route approved loans to funded lenders |
   151→| Funding structures | Supports lender-funded, Groway-funded and warehouse-funded books; a decision in Wave 0 chooses the first structure |
   152→| Reporting | Lender portfolio reports: vintages, roll rates, losses, yield, covenant tests |
   153→| Settlement | Lender and platform fee settlement via ledger accounts, reconciled to bank |
   154→
   155→### 6.8 Edge plane (SmartPoles and Digital Hubs)
   156→
   157→| Component | Specification |
   158→| --- | --- |
   159→| Runtime | K3s on a hardened hub appliance; single Helm chart bundle; installable by a non-specialist in under 30 minutes with a validation suite |
   160→| Decision cache | Signed eligibility envelopes (Ed25519 now, PQC-ready) with expiry and per-customer limits; validated locally |
   161→| Offline origination | Local queue (SQLite or Postgres) capturing applications, repayments and consent events; conflict rules on sync; offline limits are conservative and capped |
   162→| Local inference | ONNX Runtime for a simplified scorecard when the core is unreachable |
   163→| Sync | MQTT or HTTPS batching over WiFi backhaul; LTE or satellite failover where available; disk-backed buffering |
   164→| Power and health | Battery state, uptime and sync lag exported as metrics and used as a service-quality signal |
   165→| Pilot | Seshego Digital Hub first, then a second hub to prove repeatability |

   167→### 6.9 Operations and back office
   168→
   169→- Staff portal: customer 360, case management, manual review queue, limit overrides with approval workflow, dispute handling.
   170→- Finance operations: reconciliation workbench, suspense clearing, fee and lender settlement.
   171→- Regulatory reporting: NCR, FIC and jurisdiction reports generated from the reporting mart with lineage back to ledger events.
   172→- Mission Control: agent assistance for triage (document checks, reviewer summaries, collections drafts, compliance checks). Agents recommend; named staff approve. All agent actions are logged as events.
   173→
   174→### 6.10 Data platform and reporting
   175→
   176→- Event log is the integration backbone. Start with a managed or lightweight broker (Redpanda or a small Kafka cluster) when more than two consumers need ordered replay; before that, Postgres outbox plus a queue is acceptable.
   177→- Batch pipelines orchestrated by Dagster or Airflow; dbt for reporting marts; lakehouse tables for training and audit.
   178→- Data quality checks on every pipeline; reconciliation of ledger totals to marts as a daily control.
   179→
   180→### 6.11 Security architecture
   181→
   182→| Area | Specification |
   183→| --- | --- |
   184→| Encryption | TLS 1.3 in transit; AES-256 at rest; field-level encryption for PII; KMS-managed keys; HSM-backed signing keys for envelopes and tokens |
   185→| Secrets | External Secrets Operator with Vault or cloud KMS; no secrets in Git |
   186→| Network | Private endpoints, mTLS between services, network policies, egress allow-lists |
   187→| PCI scope | Card data stays with Stitch or Adumo via tokenisation; Tshepo stores tokens only; scope documented and tested |
   188→| Audit | Immutable audit table plus hash-chained export to object storage with retention and legal hold |
   189→| Crypto-agility | Algorithm identifiers on every signature and token so PQC can be introduced by rotation, not rewrite |
   190→| Testing | SAST, dependency and container scanning, SBOM, annual penetration test, pre-launch ledger and payments security review |

   225→## 9. DevOps and platform engineering
   226→
   227→**Environments.** Separate cloud accounts for development, staging, production and security tooling. Production access is break-glass with approval and recording.
   228→
   229→**Infrastructure as code.** Terraform with Terragrunt; Helm and Kustomize for applications; all changes via pull request.
   230→
   231→**CI.** GitHub Actions or GitLab CI: build, unit and contract tests, SAST, dependency scan, container scan, SBOM, signed images, policy checks with Conftest.
   232→
   233→**CD.** Argo CD (GitOps) with Argo Rollouts for canaries; automated rollback on SLO breach; database migrations through a controlled, reversible pipeline.
   234→
   235→**Testing pyramid.** Unit, contract (Pact), integration against sandboxes (Stitch, Adumo, bureau, KYC), ledger property tests (balances always net to zero), replay tests from the event log, load tests to 3x expected peak, chaos tests on broker, database and edge sync, and quarterly disaster-recovery drills.
   236→
   237→**Observability.** OpenTelemetry traces across request, features, model, policy and ledger; Prometheus and Grafana; Loki; alerting via Alertmanager to an on-call rota. Every decision trace is retained as audit evidence for the legally required period.

   241→| SLO | Target |

   243→| Decision API p99 | under 500 ms (under 200 ms embedded target after Wave 2) |

   247→| RTO | under 4 hours |
   248→| Daily ledger reconciliation | 100% completed, exceptions cleared within 2 business days |

   268→## 11. Model risk management and responsible lending
   269→
   270→**Model governance.** Each model has an owner, purpose, training data lineage, validation report, fairness assessment where lawful, monitoring plan and retirement criteria. No model reaches full traffic without an independent validation sign-off and a canary of at least one product cycle (7 days for short-term merchant products, 30 days for longer products).
   271→
   272→**Monitoring.** Drift, approval and decline rates by segment, vintage performance, early-payment default, roll rates and override rates. Breaching a threshold automatically tightens limits and alerts the credit committee.
   273→
   274→**Responsible lending guardrails.**
   275→
   276→- Starter limits are small and increase only with demonstrated repayment.
   277→- Over-indebtedness checks across Groway products and bureau data before any new offer.
   278→- Grant-linked products enforce a minimum retained amount and a cap on total deductions.
   279→- Plain-language terms, cost-of-credit disclosure and cooling-off information in the customer's language.
   280→- Hardship and restructuring path available before any escalated collections.
   281→- Quarterly outcome review of customer harm indicators (complaints, repeat rollovers, arrears curing) by the credit committee.

   283→## 12. Business case specification (bottom-up)
   284→
   285→The financial model replaces top-down market estimates. It must be built in Wave 0 and updated with pilot actuals.
   286→
   287→| Input group | Variables |
   288→| --- | --- |
   289→| Volume | Eligible customers or merchants by segment, take-up rate, average ticket, loans per year |
   290→| Credit | Probability of default by product and vintage, loss given default, recovery rate, time to write-off |
   291→| Funding | Lender cost of funds, advance rate, first-loss structure, capital required per rand of book |
   292→| Revenue | Origination fee, interest share, settlement fee, SaaS or licence fee, partner revenue share |
   293→| Costs | Cloud and edge costs, bureau and KYC cost per check, collections cost, compliance, staffing, support |
   294→| Outputs | Contribution margin per loan, breakeven default rate, cash need by month, payback, sensitivity to PD, take-up and cost of funds |
   295→
   296→The model must show a breakeven default rate per product and a downside case. Investor materials quote only figures derived from this model and from pilot data.

   455→### 18.2 Engineering standards
   456→
   457→| Area | Standard |
   458→| --- | --- |
   459→| Contracts first | Write the Protobuf or OpenAPI definition before the code; a breaking change needs a new version |
   460→| Money | Integer minor units plus ISO currency code; never floating point |
   461→| Identifiers | UUIDv7 for entities; a correlation ID on every request and event |
   462→| Time | UTC everywhere; local time only at display |
   463→| Idempotency | Every write endpoint and event consumer is idempotent by key; a duplicate is a no-op |
   464→| Outbox | A state change and its event are committed in one transaction through the outbox table |
   465→| Errors | Typed error codes; no personal data in errors or logs |
   466→| Personal data | Tokenised at ingestion; raw identifiers live only in the identity vault and KYC service |
   467→| Migrations | Forward-only, run by the pipeline before deploy; undone by a compensating migration, never by editing history |
   468→| Feature flags | Every new product, policy and model sits behind a flag scoped by tenant |
   469→| Configuration | Policy, limits and pricing are versioned data, not constants in code |
   470→| Observability | Every handler emits a trace span and the standard metrics; decision traces are retained as audit evidence |
   471→| Testing | Unit, contract and integration tests on every service; property tests on the ledger |
   472→| Review | Four-eyes review on ledger, repayment, credit policy, consent, security and contract changes |
   473→
   474→### 18.3 Rules for AI-assisted development
   475→
   476→AI tools are used for scaffolding, adapters, test generation, documentation and refactors. The rules keep that speed from weakening the controls.
   477→
   478→- Generated code passes the same pipeline and review as written code.
   479→- Named humans own and review ledger, repayment, credit policy, consent, cryptography and any migration touching money.
   480→- No secrets and no real customer data go into prompts.
   481→- Generated tests are reviewed for what they assert, not only for coverage.
   482→- A pull request that contains a substantial generated module says so and links the prompt used.

   509→### 18.5 Blueprints for the critical components
   510→
   511→**Ledger schema (illustrative).**
   512→
   513→```sql
   514→CREATE TABLE account (
   515→  id uuid PRIMARY KEY,
   516→  tenant_id uuid NOT NULL,
   517→  kind text NOT NULL,          -- customer, merchant, lender, fee, suspense, platform
   518→  currency char(3) NOT NULL,
   519→  owner_ref text NOT NULL      -- tokenised reference, never raw personal data
   520→);
   521→CREATE TABLE journal_entry (
   522→  id uuid PRIMARY KEY,
   523→  tenant_id uuid NOT NULL,
   524→  idempotency_key text NOT NULL,
   525→  posted_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
   526→  reason text NOT NULL,        -- disbursement, repayment, fee, reversal, write-off
   527→  correlation_id uuid NOT NULL,
   528→  UNIQUE (tenant_id, idempotency_key)
   529→);
   530→CREATE TABLE ledger_line (
   531→  id uuid PRIMARY KEY,
   532→  entry_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES journal_entry(id),
   533→  account_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES account(id),
   534→  amount_minor bigint NOT NULL, -- signed: debit positive, credit negative
   535→  currency char(3) NOT NULL
   536→);
   537→CREATE TABLE outbox (
   538→  id uuid PRIMARY KEY,
   539→  topic text NOT NULL,
   540→  payload bytea NOT NULL,
   541→  created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
   542→  published_at timestamptz
   543→);
   544→```
   545→
   546→Invariants to enforce and test:
   547→
   548→- The lines of every entry sum to zero per currency, enforced by a deferred constraint and by a property test.
   549→- The application role has no UPDATE or DELETE rights on `journal_entry` and `ledger_line`; a correction is a new reversing entry.
   550→- Balances are derived from lines, never stored as the source of truth.
   551→- The entry and its outbox row commit in one transaction.
   552→- Write a pending entry before calling a payment rail and settle it on confirmation, so a timeout never leaves money unrecorded.
   553→
   554→**Decision flow.**
   555→
   556→1. The gateway authenticates the caller, checks tenant and purpose, stamps a correlation ID and publishes a decision request.
   557→2. The orchestrator checks a valid consent token for every feature group it needs.
   558→3. It fetches features from the hot tier, then the warm tier; a missing critical feature means refer to review, never guess.
   559→4. Hard-stop policy runs first: KYC status, fraud flags, limits and over-indebtedness.
   560→5. The affordability assessment runs and its inputs and result are stored.
   561→6. The model runs, then policy applies the limit and pricing bands.
   562→7. Reason codes and the counterfactual text are built for every adverse outcome.
   563→8. The orchestrator emits the decision with its trace and the policy, model and feature-set versions.
   564→9. Origination presents the offer; declines above the value threshold go to the human review queue.
   565→
   566→**Edge decision envelope (illustrative).**
   567→
   568→```json
   569→{
   570→  "envelope_id": "uuidv7",
   571→  "subject": "customer-token",
   572→  "tenant": "tenant-id",
   573→  "product": "merchant-working-capital",
   574→  "max_offline_amount_minor": 50000,
   575→  "currency": "ZAR",
   576→  "valid_from": "2026-11-01T00:00:00Z",
   577→  "expires_at": "2026-11-08T00:00:00Z",
   578→  "policy_version": "p-0.3.1",
   579→  "model_version": "m-0.2.0",
   580→  "alg": "Ed25519",
   581→  "kid": "key-2026-11",
   582→  "signature": "base64"
   583→}
   584→```
   585→
   586→The edge node verifies the signature, expiry, tenant and key ID before every use, tracks offline spend locally against the cap, and reports it on sync. The core reconciles that spend and can revoke an envelope. Keep expiry short and offline caps small until the first hubs show clean reconciliation.
   587→
   588→**Repayment waterfall tests.** The order of allocation is product configuration approved by compliance. Test at least: partial payment, overpayment, early settlement, reversal of a payment, two payments arriving together, and a rail timeout followed by a late confirmation.

   635→### 18.9 Runbooks to write before launch
   636→
   637→| Runbook | First action |
   638→| --- | --- |
   639→| Ledger imbalance or failed reconciliation | Freeze new disbursements; identify the entry or rail event |

   650→### 18.10 Common build traps
   651→
   652→- Building the staff portal or the app flows before the ledger exists.
   653→- Storing a mutable balance and treating it as truth.
   654→- Treating consent as a checkbox instead of a runtime check.
   655→- Letting training features use information not available at decision time; build point-in-time features.
   656→- Sharing one database between services.
   657→- Writing policy and limits as constants in code.
   658→- Skipping idempotency on payment rail callbacks, which are delivered more than once.

   660→- Treating the pilot as a demo: it carries real customers, so every item in 18.8 applies.

   732→## 20. Alignment to the Groway source (final defining spec)
   733→
   734→This section is based on the Groway source archive and overrides sections 8, 9 and 15 where they conflict. The older Master Build file describes about 35 microservices with a React Native app. The source does not contain that: it is a single Next.js application. Tshepo is therefore built as a **modular monolith inside the Groway repository**, with clean ports so any module can be extracted later.

   737→

   741→| Development uses SQLite; the Postgres schema has only 62 models against 296 | The Postgres file is stale and unused (section 21). Convert the 296-model schema to Postgres instead; the ledger guards need Postgres |
   742→| Money is stored as floating point in GoPay, GroCredit, accounting and consumer loan models; only `ConsumerWallet` uses integer cents | Tshepo ledger uses integer minor units. Legacy float tables become read-only mirrors; new money movements are written to the ledger first |
   743→| The accounting `JournalEntry` uses floats, string dates and has no idempotency or tenant key | Keep it for accounting. Tshepo has its own ledger, linked by source type and source ID, and posts summary entries to accounting |

   820→### 21.2 Determination: production does not run the 62-model schema
   821→
   822→- The Netlify build runs `prisma generate && next build`, which reads the default schema: SQLite, 296 models. No script, Dockerfile or deployment guide refers to the Postgres file.
   823→- A SQLite file on Netlify cannot keep data between deploys and function runs, so production is effectively a demo, which matches what you described.
   824→- The Postgres file is an older design. It has 62 models, 16 of which exist nowhere else (for example Agent, Order, Commission, VirtualCard). Of the 46 it shares with the development schema, 42 differ in their fields.
   825→- 337 of the 553 route handlers that use the database depend on at least one model missing from it, including products, stock movements, consumer users, branches, audit logs and the GoPay tables.
   826→- **Decision:** retire the 62-model file. Use the 296-model schema with only the datasource changed to Postgres. Together with the six Tshepo models it is 302 models and passes the Prisma 6 validator. No migration history exists yet, so a baseline migration is created on the new database.
   827→- About 460 money and number fields are still floating point. They are not changed by this move; the ledger is the place where exact money is kept.
   828→- The database client logged every query in production and kept a client per reload. The replacement keeps one client per process and turns query logging off unless `PRISMA_LOG_QUERIES=1`. This is a plausible contributor to the lag you saw, not a confirmed cause.
   829→
