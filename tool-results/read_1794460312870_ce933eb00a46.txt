   252→### 13.4 Rail design
   253→
   254→The Tshepo mobile money rail is a provider-neutral interface, in the same ports-and-adapters style as the ledger:
   255→
   256→```ts
   257→interface MobileMoneyRail {
   258→  provider: 'pawapay' | 'paychangu';
   259→  collect(req: CollectRequest): Promise<RailAccepted>;   // customer approves on handset
   260→  payout(req: PayoutRequest): Promise<RailAccepted>;
   261→  status(reference: string): Promise<RailStatus>;
   262→  parseCallback(headers: Headers, body: string): VerifiedEvent; // throws if invalid
   263→  operators(): Promise<Operator[]>;                      // AIRTEL_MWI, TNM_MWI
   264→}
   265→
   266→type CollectRequest = {
   267→  reference: string;        // equals the ledger idempotency key
   268→  amountMinor: bigint;      // kwacha in tambala
   269→  currency: 'MWK';
   270→  msisdn: string;           // +265...
   271→  operator: 'AIRTEL_MWI' | 'TNM_MWI';
   272→  purpose: string;
   273→};
   274→```
   275→
   276→**Transaction states:** created, accepted by provider, waiting for the customer, then completed, failed, timed out, or unknown. Unknown is never treated as success: it is polled until final and reconciled.
   277→
   278→**Ledger postings (kwacha accounts kept separate from rand):**
   279→
   280→| Event | Entry |
   281→| --- | --- |
   282→| Collection completed | Debit mobile money clearing (per provider), credit merchant wallet or sales account |
   283→| Fee charged | Debit merchant wallet, credit fee income; provider cost to a cost account |
   284→| Provider settles to the kwacha bank account | Debit trust bank (kwacha), credit mobile money clearing |
   285→| Payout accepted | Debit wallet, credit payout pending (a reserve) |
   286→| Payout completed | Debit payout pending, credit mobile money clearing |
   287→| Payout failed | Reverse the reserve |
   288→
   289→Amount precision differs by provider and operator, so each provider adapter converts between tambala and the provider's decimal rules, with tests for rounding.
   290→
   291→**Reconciliation:** a daily job compares provider reports and callbacks with the ledger and sends differences to an exceptions queue. No manual edits to ledger entries; corrections are new entries.
   292→
   293→### 13.5 Callback security and failure handling
   294→
   295→- Verify every callback signature as the provider specifies, reject stale timestamps, and store the raw payload append-only.
   296→- Answer quickly with a success code and process the event asynchronously. Process each provider reference at most once.
   297→- Always have a status poll as a fallback with backoff.
   298→
   299→| Situation | Handling |
   300→| --- | --- |
   301→| Customer ignores the prompt, enters a wrong PIN or has too little money | Mark failed, show a clear message, post nothing |
   302→| No answer before the timeout | Mark unknown, poll, reconcile; never assume success |
   303→| Duplicate callback | No effect, because of the idempotency key |
   304→| Provider outage | Circuit breaker; use the second provider for the same operator; queue payouts |
   305→| Callback for an unknown reference | Quarantine and alert |
   306→| Amount differs from the request | Hold and investigate |
   307→| Wallet limit exceeded | Show the limit message; do not retry |
   308→
   309→### 13.6 Identity, limits and fraud
   310→
   311→- Validate phone numbers (+265 and operator prefixes); use account-name checks if the provider offers them.
   312→- Keep product limits inside the operator wallet limits. RBM sets transaction and balance limits for e-money accounts under the Payment Systems (E-Money) Regulations 2019.
   313→- Map KYC levels to the national ID route *VERIFY*; reuse sanctions and politically exposed person screening.
   314→- Add velocity rules, SIM-swap signals where operators share them, a cooling-off period when a payout destination changes, and dual approval for large payouts.

   421→## 16. Top-up credit: airtime, data and electricity advances (South Africa and Malawi)
   422→
   423→This section covers the product where a customer who is short of money gets airtime, data or an electricity token now, and the advance plus a fee is taken back automatically the next time they top up or buy. Public facts below were reviewed on 11 November 2026 and are listed in 16.14. Fees and menus change, so every figure here is illustrative until confirmed with the operator or vendor. *VERIFY* marks items for counsel or a partner.
   424→
   425→### 16.1 How it works in the market today
   426→
   427→**Mechanism.** The customer asks (USSD menu, SMS, app or WhatsApp). A decision engine checks recharge or purchase history and returns the amounts they qualify for. The customer sees the fee and accepts. The airtime or token is delivered immediately. The next time they recharge or buy, the platform takes the advance and fee first and lets the rest through. That automatic claw-back is the whole model, and it only works where the platform can see and intercept the top-up.
   428→
   429→**South Africa**
   430→
   431→- **Vodacom Airtime Advance** (dial *135*082#): advances of up to R20 depending on what the customer qualifies for, with an access fee of R1.10 including VAT on one Vodacom page and R1.20 per R10 or part thereof on another. Repaid from the next recharge. Top-up and uChoose customers who do not recharge are billed (the amount is added to the bill by the end of the month; another page says after 30 days). Eligibility includes recharging at least R29 a month over three months; postpaid customers do not qualify. Vodacom says more than 10 million South Africans use it.
   432→- **MTN XtraTime** launched in 2016 as a USSD service charging a small fee instead of interest. In August 2026 MTN was reported to be cutting back airtime advances while Vodacom and Telkom lean on them.
   433→- **Cell C** offers an advance for a fixed fee per transaction with no interest.
   434→- **Optasia** is reported as the company behind the airtime lending of Vodacom and MTN, and in September 2026 was reported to be acquiring Finergi, whose technology lets prepaid electricity meters work as digital wallets with repayment at the next purchase.
   435→- **Electricity:** Blu Label offers BlueAdvance for airtime and electricity; Citiq launched an in-app electricity advance in November 2025 that takes 50% of each later purchase; WattWallet is a pay-at-month-end pilot.
   436→
   437→**Malawi**
   438→
   439→- **TNM Pasavute:** airtime credit via \*600# or an SMS, with a 10% service fee repaid on the next recharge, built with South Africa's Net1 and Supa Pesa; reported at more than 20 million requests.
   440→- **Airtel Kutapa:** borrow K50 to K2,000 with codes such as *533*50#; borrowing K50 means paying back K5 extra; needs 90 days on the network, prepaid only, and no outstanding balance.
   441→- **Airtel Kutchova:** a digital loan issued by a bank and transacted on Airtel Money; borrowers use it for airtime, food and electricity.
   442→- **TNM Mpamba Ndikankhe:** an overdraft on the Mpamba wallet after 90 days of use; agents can sell ESCOM tokens and airtime.
   443→
   444→**What this means.** Both countries already have incumbents inside the operators' own recharge systems. Groway cannot simply add a menu item to an operator's USSD flow without an operator agreement. The design below therefore starts where Groway controls the top-up.

   466→### 16.3 Three ways Groway can offer it
   467→
   468→| Model | How it works | Needs | Verdict |
   469→| --- | --- | --- | --- |
   470→| **C: Merchant float advance** | Spaza merchants who sell airtime and electricity get float on credit, repaid from their sales and vending commissions | Merchant OS data, GroCredit, the ledger | Start here: you see every sale |
   471→| **B: Channel-embedded advance** | Customers buying airtime, data or electricity through Groway's own channels (Merchant OS tills, WhatsApp, SmartPole portal, wallet, Malawi mobile money) get an advance repaid on their next purchase through those channels | A vending or aggregator agreement; consent; licensing route | Second: feasible without an operator deal, but recovery only happens where Groway sees the purchase |

   476→### 16.4 End-to-end flows
   477→
   478→**Airtime or data advance (South Africa and Malawi)**
   479→
   480→1. The customer asks through WhatsApp, the till, the portal or USSD (Malawi).
   481→2. Identify the subject (phone number) and check consent for credit use.
   482→3. Check eligibility and the limit; show the menu of amounts the customer qualifies for.
   483→4. Show the quote: amount, fee and total to repay. The customer accepts.
   484→5. Post the issue entry and call the vending adapter to deliver the airtime.
   485→6. On the next top-up through a Groway channel, the top-up event calls recovery. The platform takes the allowed amount, tells the vending adapter how much to pass through, posts the entry and sends a message.
   486→7. If not recovered by the due date: add to bill where an operator bill exists (South Africa top-up customers), otherwise write off after reminders and offers.
   487→
   488→**Electricity advance (meter-anchored)**
   489→
   490→1. The customer gives the meter number. The vending provider validates the meter and supply details.
   491→2. Eligibility uses purchase history for that meter and the customer's identity, so a meter changing hands does not inherit credit.
   492→3. Quote and accept. The vendor issues a single-use token on credit and it is sent by message.
   493→4. The advance is anchored to the meter number and the customer. On each later purchase through Groway's vending path, a capped share (for example 50%) goes to the advance until repaid.
   494→5. Where a municipality allocates part of a purchase to arrears, Groway's share is taken after those deductions. *VERIFY* the allocation order with the vending provider.
   495→
   496→**Electricity recovery across channels.** A customer can buy tokens at banks, retailers and apps. The debt follows the meter only if every vending channel calls Groway's recovery hook. Options, in order: become a vendor so purchases flow through Groway; agree a deduction hook with a large vending network (one report says Blu Label's platform handles over 60% of top-ups); offer a customer-approved wallet or mobile money repayment; and size advances to what Groway can recover through its own channels. Do not promise customers that repayment is automatic everywhere.
   497→
   498→**Merchant float advance**
   499→
   500→1. A merchant buying airtime or electricity stock requests float in Merchant OS.
   501→2. GroCredit tier and sales history set the limit.
   502→3. The float is delivered; repayment is taken as a share of the merchant's later vending sales or settlements.
   503→
   504→**Malawi variants**
   505→
   506→- Airtime and ESCOM tokens are bought through a licensed provider's bill and airtime interfaces (for example PayChangu's bills and airtime endpoints).
   507→- Recovery uses the mobile money payment prompt flow from section 13: when the customer next pays through Groway, the platform deducts the advance first.
   508→- Menus are in kwacha with tambala precision. Messages are in English and Chichewa.

   522→

   545→### 16.7 Funding and economics
   546→
   547→- **Float funding:** someone pays the supplier for the airtime or electricity delivered before the customer repays. Options: lender-funded, Groway-funded, or a supplier credit line. This is a Wave 0 decision.
   548→- **Fee as a rate:** a fee of R2.40 on a R20 advance repaid in 7 days is 12% for the week. Short terms make fees look small and annual rates very high. Report both to the credit committee and regulators.
   549→- **Loss drivers:** customers who stop using the number or meter, switch SIMs, or buy through channels Groway does not see. Track recovery rate, days to recover, and net loss by cohort and channel.
   550→- **Unit economics model:** advance volume, take-up, fee, recovery rate, funding cost, supplier margin, messaging and vending costs, fraud losses. Build it before launch and keep it updated with pilot data.

   562→
   563→- Small starter limits that rise only with repayment.

   570→### 16.9 Fraud and abuse controls
   571→
   572→- Phone number recycling and SIM swap: require tenure on the current SIM where the operator shares it; pause advances after a swap.
   573→- Advance farming: link accounts by device, identity and payment instrument; block repeat patterns.
   574→- Meter sharing and meter changes: bind eligibility to the meter and the verified customer.
   575→- Agent and merchant collusion: velocity rules and dual approval for large float.
