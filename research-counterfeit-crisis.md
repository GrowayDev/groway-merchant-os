## The Child Deaths (2024)

- **Verified death toll: 22–23 children nationwide in 2024** (the user's "over 20" figure is correct).
  - **22 children** — official figure cited by **President Cyril Ramaphosa** in his national address on **15 November 2024** ("Over the last few weeks alone, food-borne illnesses have claimed the lives of at least 22 of our nation's children"). Source: The Presidency, Republic of South Africa [3].
  - **23 children** — figure used by **New York Times (24 Nov 2024)** [1], **France24 (15 Nov 2024)** [6], **SABC News**, **Beyond Pesticides (19 Dec 2024)** [9], and **Mail & Guardian (28 Aug 2025)** [5]. The marginal increase reflects cases confirmed after the President's address.
- **Illnesses:** Since **1 September 2024**, **890 reported food-borne illness incidents** across all provinces (Presidency [3]).
- **Most-affected provinces:** Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal, with Limpopo, Free State and Mpumalanga also recording dozens of incidents (Presidency [3]).
- **Named township / incident clusters:**
  - **Naledi, Soweto (Gauteng)** — 6 children died in October 2024 after eating snacks bought from a local spaza shop; the youngest was **6 years old**. Confirmed cause: **Terbufos** (an organophosphate pesticide) ingestion, established by **NICD** lab analysis (SAnews.gov.za, 28 Oct 2024 [10]; Reuters, 28 Oct 2024 [4]; Health Minister Aaron Motsoaledi briefing).
  - **Ekurhuleni (Gauteng)** and **Soweto** — in **2023**, 3 children in Ekurhuleni and 3 children in Soweto died from exposure to **Aldicarb** (Presidency [3]).
  - Other 2024 cases spread across Gauteng, KZN, Limpopo, Free State, Mpumalanga.
- **Root cause (verified):**
  - The 6 Naledi deaths were caused by **Terbufos**, an **organophosphate** agricultural pesticide **registered in SA only for agricultural use** and **not allowed for household use**, but informally sold as a "street pesticide" for rat control in townships (Presidency [3]; SAnews [10]).
  - A chip packet found on one of the deceased Naledi children had **Terbufos traces on both the inside and the outside** of the packet. Of **84 spaza shops inspected in Naledi, 3 had evidence of Terbufos** (Presidency [3]).
  - Other street pesticides in circulation: **Aldicarb** (banned in SA since 2016) and **Galephirimi** (Presidency [3]).
  - **4 arrests** were made at a Johannesburg mall for selling **Aldicarb**; **1,450 g** confiscated. Spaza-shop owners identified the sellers (SAnews [10]).
  - Inspectors found food stored alongside pesticides and detergents, and shops lacking proper food storage and hand/dishwashing facilities (Presidency [3]).
- **Note on "counterfeit" framing:** The 2024 child deaths were caused primarily by **street-pesticide contamination of food/snacks sold in spaza shops**, not by "counterfeit food" in the strict trademark sense. However, the **NYT [1]**, **France24 [6]** and **ActionSA [2]** explicitly link the crisis to "expired and counterfeit food products" and "counterfeit goods" as part of the broader spaza-shop supply-chain integrity problem. The user's framing of "counterfeit goods that killed children" is **directionally supported** by the spaza-shop distribution channel, but the proximate cause of death was pesticide contamination, not trademark counterfeiting. Be precise in the video voice-over.

## Regulatory Response (2024)

Announced by **President Ramaphosa, 15 November 2024** (Presidency [3]) and refined through to Feb 2025:

- **Immediate closure** of all spaza shops implicated in child deaths.
- **21-day registration deadline** for **all spaza shops and food-handling facilities** to register with their municipalities — announced 15 Nov 2024, original deadline **17 December 2024** (COGTA [12]; gov.za [13]), subsequently **extended to 28 February 2025** (SAnews [14]; gov.za Inter-Ministerial Committee, 28 Feb 2025 [15]). Shops not registered or non-compliant with health standards to be shut down.
- **Door-to-door inspection campaign** of all spaza shops, tuck shops and informal traders, starting in Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal, conducted by **SAPS, SANDF Military Health Services, environmental health practitioners, the National Consumer Commission, and labour inspectors**. Initial phase targeted for completion within one month.
- **Joint Operational and Intelligence Structures (JOINTS)** established at national and provincial level.
- **All registered Terbufos manufacturers** to be inspected to prevent diversion into the non-agricultural market; supply-chain controls to be audited.
- **All deaths of patients 12 years and under** made notifiable under the Notifiable Medical Conditions Surveillance System.
- **Electronic medical certification of death** system to be established for real-time cause-of-death reporting to the National Department of Health.
- **R500 million joint fund** established by the **Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (dtic)** and the **Department of Small Business Development (DSBD)** to support township and rural businesses, including spaza shops, with refurbishment, technical skills, regulatory compliance and capacity building (Presidency [3]; UCT Case Writing Centre [16]).
- **Ministerial Health Advisory Committee** established (toxicologists, paediatricians, epidemiologists, forensic pathologists) to develop medium- and long-term prevention measures.

**SAPS Counterfeit Goods enforcement (separate, ongoing):**
- The **SAPS National Counterfeit and Illicit Goods Unit** conducts nationwide raids. Recent operations seized counterfeit goods worth **R17 million+ (16 suspects arrested)**, **R20 million+ in Bruma**, and **R28 million+ including label-printing machines in a separate KZN takedown** (SAPS social media [17][18][19]).
- The **Counterfeit Goods Act 37 of 1997** creates the offences and empowers inspectors and SAPS members to enter premises, search, and seize counterfeit goods (gov.za [20]). **Section 15** deals with the powers of **SARS Customs & Excise** to seize and detain counterfeit or suspected counterfeit goods at ports of entry (Lexology; SARS SC-CC-28 External Policy [21][22]).
- **SARS Customs & Excise** and the **National Liquor Authority** jointly enforce against illicit alcohol and tobacco (Carte Blanche; TRACIT [7]).

**SAHPRA (medicines regulator):**
- Operates a **Rapid Alert System** to communicate detection of substandard or falsified medicines (SAHPRA [23]).
- Recent alerts include **falsified Ozempic/semaglutide** products (SAHPRA [24]).
- South Africa launched the **continent's first National Action Plan to fight falsified and substandard medical products** (eNCA [25]).

## The Spaza-Shop Distribution Problem

- **~150,000 spaza shops** operate across South Africa (UCT Case Writing Centre, citing RogerWilco 2021 Township Marketing Report [16]; corroborated by IRR [39]).
- Spaza shops contribute **~5.2% to South Africa's GDP** and **employ ~2.6 million people** [16][39].
- The broader **township economy is estimated at ~R900 billion**, with the informal retail (spaza) sector its largest contributor [16].
- Spaza retail economy estimates range from **~R100 bn to ~R200 bn annually** (LinkedIn / industry estimates [40][41]).
- A **R500 million Spaza Shop Support Fund** was launched by DSBD + dtic in 2024 to support SA-owned spaza shops with financial and non-financial support [16].
- **Why spaza shops are the choke point:**
  1. They are the **last-mile distributor** of fast-moving consumer goods (snacks, baby food, paracetamol, energy drinks, cosmetics) to **tens of millions of low-income South Africans** — often the **only retail option** in townships and informal settlements [16].
  2. They typically source from **informal cash-and-carry wholesalers** with **no provenance records**, no batch tracking, no electronic invoicing — making it impossible to trace a tainted product back to its wholesaler/manufacturer after a death or illness.
  3. Spaza owners **cannot authenticate stock at point of purchase** — they have no scanner, no POS integration with manufacturer registries, and no way to distinguish real from fake branded goods.
  4. Inspectors found food stored alongside pesticides and detergents, and shops lacking safe food storage and hand/dishwashing facilities (Presidency [3]).
  5. **Many municipalities lack the capacity and resources** to conduct environmental health inspections of spaza shops (Presidency [3]) — the regulatory enforcement layer is structurally thin.

## Why Current Solutions Fail

- **No supply-chain traceability at POS.** Spaza shops record purchases in paper notebooks or not at all. When a child dies, the only evidence is a chip packet with Terbufos residue (Presidency [3]) — there is no electronic audit trail from retailer → wholesaler → distributor → manufacturer.
- **Spaza owners cannot authenticate stock at point of purchase.** There is no nationwide product-authentication mechanism (no QR, no GS1 2D barcode verification, no SAHPRA-registry lookup) integrated into the till.
- **Slow regulator notification.** The Naledi deaths occurred in **early October 2024**; the NICD lab confirmation came only on **28 October 2024** — **~3 weeks** of latency before national action [10]. The President's national address was **15 November 2024** — **~6 weeks** after the Naledi incident.
- **Reactive, not preventive.** The Counterfeit Goods Act 37 of 1997 empowers seizure **after** counterfeit goods are already on the market [20]. SAPS raids net R17–R28 million per operation [17][18][19], but this is a fraction of the **R100 bn/year** illicit market [26].
- **Municipal inspection capacity is structurally thin** (Presidency [3]) — only ~80 environmental health practitioners were deployable to Naledi [10].
- **Customs enforcement stops at the border; spaza shops operate inland** with no real-time data feed back to SARS / SAHPRA / the National Consumer Commission.
- **No prosecutions 12+ months on** — SABC News "Case In Point" (2025) headline: *"Why are there still no prosecutions for 23 children who died after consuming spaza shop snacks in 2024?"* [42] — illustrating the traceability and evidentiary gap.

## Verifiable Statement Connecting Counterfeit / Unsafe Goods to Child Mortality & the Township Economy

> "Over the last few weeks alone, food-borne illnesses have claimed the lives of at least 22 of our nation's children … The deaths of the six children in Naledi, Soweto can be directly attributed to a highly hazardous chemical used as a pesticide known as Terbufos … Terbufos is being informally sold as a so-called 'street pesticide' for domestic use in townships and informal settlements … The unregulated use of restricted pesticides in communities has become a growing problem, with devastating consequences."
> — **President Cyril Ramaphosa, 15 November 2024** [3]

> "23 children in South Africa died and nearly 900 people sickened from food contaminated with 'street pesticides' in Johannesburg's Soweto."
> — **Beyond Pesticides, 19 December 2024** [9]

> "South Africa suffers 'staggering losses' of around R100 billion a year due to illicit trade [and] counterfeiting."
> — **TRACIT (Transnational Alliance to Combat Illicit Trade)** [26]

> "There are an estimated 150,000 spaza shops operating across the country that contribute around 5.2% to South Africa's GDP and employ 2.6 million people."
> — **UCT Case Writing Centre** [16]

These four quotes, taken together, establish the **direct causal chain** that the Groway Merchant OS / SAPE module addresses:

**Untraceable, unsafe / counterfeit / contaminated goods** → **distributed through 150,000 spaza shops with no POS-level authentication or traceability** → **consumed by township children** → **22+ child deaths in 2024 + ~R100 bn/year in illicit-trade economic loss.**

- **No SA-specific 2024 SAHPRA counterfeit-medicine seizure figure** (units/doses) was located in the searches. The "1 million units of illegal medicines detained in 2024" figure that appears in some results refers to **Ireland's HPRA**, not SAHPRA. SAHPRA operates a Rapid Alert System and issued a falsified-Ozempic alert, but a 2024 SAHPRA-specific counterfeit-medicine seizure volume could not be sourced.
- **No SA-specific 2024 counterfeit baby-formula case** was located. Search results for "counterfeit baby formula" returned China (2004, 2016, 2017), Spain, Malaysia, Malawi and US cases — none South African in 2024. The video script should not assert that counterfeit baby formula killed children in SA in 2024.
- The 22-vs-23 child-death discrepancy: **22** is the President's official Nov 15 figure; **23** is the consolidated figure used by NYT/France24/SABC/Beyond Pesticides/M&G after the President's address. Use **"at least 22, with reporting consolidating at 23"** for precision.
