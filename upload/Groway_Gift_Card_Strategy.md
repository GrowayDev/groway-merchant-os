**Key Structural Shifts Driving Revised Forecasts**

The single most significant structural change since the 2024 report is
the transition from physical card dominance to what the February 2026
databook terms \'app-based issuance and loyalty-linked stored value.\'
This shift is driven by three concurrent forces: the SARB\'s Payment
Ecosystem Modernisation (PEM) program opened real-time rails to non-bank
fintechs; major retailers (Shoprite, Pick n Pay, Woolworths) have
embedded digital gift cards into their mobile apps and loyalty
ecosystems; and fintech disruptors (Stitch, Ozow, MTN MoMo) have made
API-based stored value integration technically and commercially
accessible within weeks rather than months.

For Groway, this means the window to capture meaningful market share as
a digital-first gift card distributor through API partnerships is open
now --- and is likely to narrow as competitive intensity increases
through 2027.

**2.3 The Mobile-First Opportunity**

Android devices account for over 82% of SA smartphone OS share.
Entry-level handsets below ZAR 1,500 have democratised access to
first-time internet users in Soweto, Khayelitsha, and Umlazi --- the
same demographics gift card programs have struggled to reach with
physical plastic. For Groway\'s Consumer Wallet, this translates
directly: the gift card use case (send, receive, redeem) is well-suited
to a lightweight mobile-first UX, and the absence of international card
credentials among this segment makes gift cards a natural bridge to
global digital services (gaming, streaming, e-commerce).

**3. Competitive Landscape**

The SA gift card market is consolidating around three tiers: dominant
closed-loop retail programs (Shoprite, Pick n Pay, Woolworths),
specialist incentive platforms (Innervation, DigiCard, Creative
Incentives), and emerging fintech distributors (Flutterwave, Chipper
Cash, Bitrefill). The February 2026 databook notes that \'competitive
intensity is expected to increase as digital distribution becomes table
stakes and differentiation shifts toward ecosystem integration.\'
Groway\'s window of advantage lies in being the first consumer wallet
and merchant OS to offer a multi-brand gift card catalog embedded in a
transactional ecosystem, rather than a standalone gift card product.

**Technical Integration Considerations**

-   All recommended APIs are REST-based with sandbox environments ---
    Groway\'s engineering team can validate integration without
    production risk

-   Gift card issuance and redemption must be event-sourced within
    Groway\'s wallet ledger --- track load, purchase, transfer, and
    redemption as separate ledger events to support breakage accounting
    and regulatory reporting

-   PASA compliance note: As long as Groway uses a licensed provider\'s
    infrastructure (Reloadly, InComm), Groway itself does not require
    independent PASA certification at launch --- confirm with legal
    counsel

-   Fraud controls: Implement velocity limits (e.g., max 3 gift card
    purchases per user per day), device fingerprinting, and OTP on
    high-value purchases (\>R500) from Day 1 --- industry-wide
    bot/enumeration attacks are escalating

-   3-year expiry: July 2024 SA regulation mandates minimum 3-year
    expiry from issuance or last reload --- build this into the wallet
    ledger and consumer-facing UX with clear expiry notifications

**7. Regulatory Environment**

**7.1 Key Regulations Affecting Groway\'s Gift Card Integration**

-   July 2024 SA Gift Card Regulation: Minimum 3-year expiry period from
    date of issuance or last reload; enhanced transparency requirements;
    stricter AML compliance. This is now law --- build into ledger from
    Day 1.

-   PASA Licensing: Electronic money transfer services require
    licensing; 6--12 month certification timeline. Groway avoids this by
    using licensed API partners. Confirm with legal counsel whether
    Groway\'s wallet activities independently trigger PASA obligations.

-   SARB Vision 2025 & PEM Program: National Payment System
    modernisation; explicit goal of 90% electronic transaction
    penetration by mid-decade; opened NPS to fintechs August 2025.
    Directly enables Groway\'s digital gift card strategy.

-   FSCA Oversight: Financial Sector Conduct Authority regulates
    consumer protection; gift card consumer disclosure requirements must
    be embedded in Groway\'s UX.

-   NCA Applicability: National Credit Act does not currently apply to
    BNPL --- enables flexible bundle products, but regulatory ambiguity
    should be monitored.

**7.2 Compliance Actions Required**

> **Immediate:** Confirm PASA licensing exposure with SA legal counsel
> before launch; document API partner licensing status
>
> **Pre-Launch:** Build 3-year expiry tracking and consumer notification
> system into wallet ledger; implement AML transaction monitoring at
> gift card purchase and redemption events
>
> **Ongoing:** FSCA consumer disclosure compliance in UX (expiry dates,
> fees, redemption restrictions); quarterly regulatory monitoring for
> NPS/SARB changes

**11. Conclusion & Strategic Recommendation**

The South African gift card market is at an inflection point. The
structural convergence of an NPS opened to fintechs, PayShap QR payments
arriving in early 2026, explosive digital wallet growth (Capitec 159%
txn volume increase; FNB R98B transaction value), and a market
trajectory to USD 2.46 billion by 2030 creates a clear commercial
opportunity for Groway.

The case for inclusion in Groway\'s Consumer Wallet and Merchant OS
service portfolio is strong across three dimensions: revenue
diversification (R5M--20M Year 1 depending on execution), user
engagement (gift card purchase is a high-frequency, high-intent action
that drives wallet stickiness), and merchant value-add (SME merchants on
Merchant OS gain access to a gifting and incentive capability previously
only available to large retailers).
